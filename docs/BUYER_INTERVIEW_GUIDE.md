# RESOLVEGRAPH User Research — Buyer Interview Guide

## Study Title
Evaluating Consumer Perceptions, Trust, and Transparency in AI-Assisted Commerce Dispute Resolution.

## Participant Profile
- **Target Audience**: Consumers who have filed at least one commercial dispute, chargeback, or PayPal resolution claim within the past 12 months.
- **Session Duration**: 45 minutes.
- **Format**: Semi-structured 1-on-1 qualitative interview + interactive walkthrough of RESOLVEGRAPH Buyer Portal.

---

## 1. Introduction & Informed Consent (5 minutes)
- Welcoming remarks and introduction of researcher role.
- Explanation of study purpose: "We are researching how people experience online purchase disputes and how transparent, evidence-based tools can make resolutions faster and fairer."
- Reassurance that participation is voluntary, responses are confidential, and no real financial credentials will be collected.
- Recording consent confirmation.

---

## 2. Past Dispute Experience & Pain Points (10 minutes)
1. **Background**: "Can you walk me through the most recent time an online purchase went wrong (e.g., wrong item, damaged delivery, or billing issue)?"
2. **Communication**: "How did you first attempt to communicate with the merchant? What went well, and what felt frustrating?"
3. **Evidence Submission**: "When you were asked for evidence (photos, receipts, packaging), what was that experience like? Did you feel the seller or platform actually reviewed what you sent?"
4. **Perception of Fairness**: "Did the final decision feel fair to you? Why or why not? Did you understand *how* the decision was reached?"
5. **Chargeback / Escalation**: "Did you feel forced to escalate to PayPal or file a credit card chargeback? What made that feel necessary?"

---

## 3. Walkthrough: RESOLVEGRAPH Consumer Portal (20 minutes)
*Provide participant with interactive staging access to the RESOLVEGRAPH Buyer Dispute View.*

### Task A: Initial Case Overview & Status Understanding
- *Prompt*: "Imagine you ordered an SSD and received the wrong model. Look at this screen for 30 seconds. In your own words, what is happening with your dispute right now?"
- *Observations to note*:
  - Do they notice the plain-language status explanation?
  - Do they understand the distinction between "Under Review", "Evidence Requested", and "Proposal Ready"?

### Task B: Interacting with the Bilateral Evidence Graph
- *Prompt*: "Explore the 'Evidence & Claims' section. Click on the connections between your statements and the documents."
- *Questions*:
  - "Does seeing the merchant's documentation side-by-side with yours feel helpful or stressful?"
  - "Do you trust the system more when it shows you exactly which line in the invoice or tracking slip is being cited?"
  - "What does the 'Discrepancy Score' or 'Match' indicator mean to you?"

### Task C: Reviewing a Proposed Remedy & Copilot Assistance
- *Prompt*: "The merchant has offered a partial refund or replacement. Review this proposal."
- *Questions*:
  - "Is the explanation clear on why this specific remedy was suggested?"
  - "How do you feel about an AI assistant drafting the explanation, knowing that a human operator still approves the money transfer?"
  - "Would you accept this proposal, or would you escalate to PayPal? What would make you decide?"

---

## 4. Debrief & Quantitative Rating (10 minutes)
Ask participant to rate the following on a 1–5 Likert scale (1 = Strongly Disagree, 5 = Strongly Agree):
1. *"I understand why the system made its recommendation."*
2. *"I feel my evidence was evaluated as fairly as the merchant's evidence."*
3. *"I would prefer this platform over traditional multi-week email support."*
4. *"Knowing that financial actions require human sign-off increases my trust in the AI."*

**Final Question**:
- "If you could change one thing about how this dispute was handled, what would it be?"
